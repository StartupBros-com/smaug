Lenny Rachitsky interviews Cat Wu, Head of Product for Claude Code, about the operating model behind Anthropic’s rapid product cadence. Wu argues that AI makes engineering less expensive and model capabilities faster-moving, so AI-native product teams need crisp user and use-case goals, tight launch loops, and people with product taste who can execute across traditional role boundaries.

The Claude Code team uses research previews to learn from users without making large upfront commitments. It combines weekly metrics readouts, explicit team principles, and a lightweight handoff process among engineering, documentation, marketing, and developer relations, enabling an internally dogfooded feature to reach users quickly. Wu also frames product management as increasingly intertwined with engineering and design rather than a separate coordination layer.

## Key Takeaways

- AI-native PMs should shorten the time from a product idea to user feedback, rather than optimize only for multi-quarter coordination.
- Clear definitions of the user, the key task, and acceptable tradeoffs help autonomous engineers make decisions without waiting on product approval.
- Research previews lower the commitment required to ship an early capability and create a faster learning loop with users.
- Shared metrics, team principles, and a predictable engineering-to-docs-to-marketing handoff remove common launch friction.
- As code becomes cheaper to produce, product taste—choosing what to build and how users should experience it—becomes more valuable across PM, engineering, and design roles.
