Josh Woodward explains how Google Labs operates as a small-team, 0-to-1 product organization inside Google. Its teams work from strategic areas such as software development or creativity, then pursue specific user problems quickly, aiming to get an idea into users' hands within roughly 50 to 100 days. He emphasizes that early products should be judged against small but meaningful signals, such as 10,000 weekly active users, rather than the scale expected of mature Google products.

Woodward also argues that prompt boxes are an interim interface. Developers may keep writing long structured prompts, but most users will provide context through documents, images, voice, and video instead. On generative video, he sees image quality, physics, and instruction following improving rapidly while controllable character consistency, cost, and workflow-level applications remain central opportunities.

## Key Takeaways

- Small cross-functional teams can move faster when they pick strategic domains centrally but discover individual user problems locally.
- Product strategy should benefit directly from models becoming more capable, faster, and cheaper over time.
- Multimodal context is likely to replace much of end-user prompt writing, even as complex prompting remains useful for engineers.
- Generative video is advancing from novelty toward workflows, with controllability and unit economics now as important as visual fidelity.
