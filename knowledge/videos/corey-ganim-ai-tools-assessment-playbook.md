Corey Ganim explains a productized AI-tools assessment for small businesses with roughly two to 20 employees. The $999 offer begins with a 45-minute discovery conversation to identify bottlenecks, then recommends three to seven off-the-shelf tools intended to reclaim at least five hours per week; he says the purchase is refundable if that threshold cannot be identified. The assessment is designed as a low-friction entry offer that establishes trust before an implementation engagement.

The four-step delivery process is discovery, AI-assisted analysis of the recorded call, a client-facing report, and a review call. Ganim uses a custom Claude skill to research candidate tools from the discovery transcript, but emphasizes human review to avoid unsuitable recommendations. The report prioritizes low-effort, high-impact quick wins, quantifies estimated ROI, and includes a short implementation plan that prevents a client from being overwhelmed by the list of recommendations.

He positions the completed assessment as evidence for higher-value work: process redesign, simple automation, knowledge systems, custom skills and workflows, or broader implementations. His recurring "AI concierge" offer provides two working sessions a month to help a client use Claude and build skills; its onboarding connects tools, creates context files and global instructions, and teaches the client how to turn a workflow into a repeatable skill.

## Key Takeaways

- Sell a tightly scoped assessment that guarantees a concrete outcome, rather than leading with a vague AI-transformation engagement.
- Record the discovery call, use its transcript to surface tool recommendations, then review every suggestion for the client's size and context.
- A concise report should connect each pain point to a specific tool, setup effort, expected time savings, and a prioritized quick-start plan.
- The assessment both identifies implementation projects and reduces the sales friction for follow-on work because the client has already seen a credible opportunity.
- Source initial clients through local AI meetups, direct outreach, free mini-assessments, referral partners, coworking-space office hours, and public proof of small wins.
- Specializing by geography or industry can increase trust, clarify the offer, and reduce price pressure for an AI-service provider.
