Ashlee Vance and Kylie Robison interview OpenAI co-founders Sam Altman and Greg Brockman in an episode of Core Memory. In the opening portion, the guests describe how OpenAI emerged from a 2015 discussion about building an AGI lab, and how their complementary working styles—Altman’s emphasis on ambition and connections, Brockman’s focus on priorities and execution—have remained central to the company.

They describe AI safety as an operational and societal problem rather than a single technical intervention. Their stated approach favors iterative deployment: putting progressively capable systems into real use, learning from their effects, and preparing people and institutions alongside the technology. They also argue that public discussion should extend beyond technical capability toward prosperity, human agency, meaningful work, and the practical ways people already use ChatGPT.

## Key Takeaways

- Altman and Brockman portray constant communication and complementary perspectives as a durable co-founder operating model.
- They frame iterative deployment as part of safety: real-world use and feedback should accompany rising model capability.
- The discussion treats societal resilience, economic agency, and meaningful work as core questions for AI deployment.
- They argue that AI’s benefits are easier to see through direct use, including research, advocacy, and entrepreneurial applications.
