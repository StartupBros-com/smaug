Lenny Rachitsky interviews Fiona Fung, who leads the Claude Code and Cowork teams at Anthropic, about operating product and engineering organizations after AI greatly increases code throughput. Fung’s central claim is that coding is no longer the primary constraint: the harder problem is verifying that rapidly shipped work delivers the intended experience. She describes using feedback channels, automated routines, and explicit quality frameworks to keep product learning aligned with output.

The conversation also covers the organizational consequences of agent-assisted work. Fung argues for maintaining team connection through pairwise programming lunches, keeping planning short-horizon and disposable when it stops helping, and building a culture that raises problems early. Her management approach combines autonomous teams with accountability for the hypothesis, outcome, and user experience behind what they ship.

## Key Takeaways

- AI-assisted throughput makes verification, feedback synthesis, and product quality more important bottlenecks than implementation speed.
- Track both unrecoverable failures and recoverable frustrations; user swearing can expose dissatisfaction that conventional evaluations miss.
- Automated routines can collect feedback across channels, identify recurring themes, and prepare candidate fixes for review rather than requiring a manager to triage manually each morning.
- Put explicit definitions of quality and current specifications in the repository so automated review can validate work against a shared standard.
- Pairwise programming lunches provide lightweight social learning and counter the isolation of engineers working independently with agents.
- Monthly, just-in-time planning and permission to retire ineffective processes fit a fast-changing product environment better than unused long-range roadmaps.
