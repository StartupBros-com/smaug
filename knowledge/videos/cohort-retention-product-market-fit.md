David Lieb explains cohort retention as the clearest quantitative test of whether a startup has made something people want. Rather than combining all users into a single activity metric, founders should group users by when they first used the product and measure which fraction returns in each subsequent period to perform an action that demonstrates real value.

The central signal is the shape of the retention curve. A curve that eventually flattens shows that a durable group of users remains and can accumulate over time; a curve that continually declines means new acquisition only replaces churn. Lieb illustrates this with Google Photos, where a stable weekly-retention plateau gave early confidence despite substantial initial drop-off.

## Key Takeaways

- Define cohorts by a first-use period, then choose a value-bearing action rather than a shallow event such as an app open or notification click.
- Set the measurement interval to the product’s intended use cadence: daily for entertainment, weekly for utilities, and longer only when the product naturally warrants it.
- Evaluate the full retention-curve shape, especially whether it flattens, instead of celebrating an isolated retention percentage.
- Avoid flattering metrics created by overly broad time windows, easy activity definitions, or payment status without evidence of actual product use.
