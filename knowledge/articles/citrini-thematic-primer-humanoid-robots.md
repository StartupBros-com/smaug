Citrini's humanoid-robots primer (their most comprehensive thematic work since the 2023 AI primer). Core thesis: humanoid robots are a **secular growth story available at cyclical-trough prices**. The main beneficiaries are advanced machinery/manufacturing suppliers concentrated in automotive and defense — sectors bombed out by a brutal auto cycle and (then-current) DOGE-driven defense-spending fears. Analyst estimates on these names assume the trough persists forever, pricing in no cyclical recovery, let alone a robot-shaped right tail (Texas Instruments had to remind analysts that analog semi cycles do bottom). The play is a picks-and-shovels basket of names with robotics upside, scaled in *before* the consumer-robot "ChatGPT moment," which they see as visible and nearer than consensus — a "3-4 years" thing, not "next decade." Their stated risk of waiting: the auto cycle bottoms and defense re-rates first, destroying the cyclical entry price.

## Why Humanoid?

- The world is already ergonomically tooled for the human form (two arms, ten digits, 95th-percentile reach) — no retrofitting, no new conveyance, drop a unit into an existing BMW body cell and it uses the night shift's air ratchet. One device generalizes across dishes/floors/driving only if it's humanoid.
- Deployment path mirrors the 2010 Kiva→Amazon pivot: warehouses first (Amazon piloting Agility's Digit; GXO + Apptronik running pick-and-place pilots for ~a year), then along the supply chain, then service → domestic.
- **Full autonomy is not required** (the contrarian bit): teleoperation bridges the gap. With current automation + NVIDIA's robotics training, one tele-operator in India can "coach" 4-5 robots in the US, intervening only in unstructured edge cases — slashing developed-market per-task labor costs *before* autonomy improves. Economic echo of 2000-era call-center offshoring, "only this time the headset controls a spine." Human-in-the-loop work also generates the training data that eventually removes the human.

## Why Now? Three answers

1. **The macro (cyclical entry):** auto cycle at the bottom, defense sold off on overblown fiscal fears, consumer-electronics woes — all suppressing the exact names with robotics exposure. Commentary from RRX, MCHP, ON, IFX suggests the downtrend has bottomed: inventory purge over, so every incremental unit of sensor/actuator/inverter demand drops straight to the P&L. Tariff newsflow mostly positive; CEOs pricing EBITDA-neutral mitigation (RRX cited). Parallel drawn to capex-anemic semis being reinvigorated by the AI theme in 2022.
2. **The cost curve:** Unitree's G-1 humanoid ships at **$16k sticker — a 97% slide from the ~$500k industrial units of 2023** — evidence robots are slipping onto a Moore-style cost curve. Consumer tipping point ≈ cost below a used Honda Civic, or Robotics-as-a-Service at ~$500/month for chores; brown-field retail/light-industrial sites desperate for robotic workers under $25/hour come first. (Author personally verifies Unitree's robots are real, contra CGI claims; <$20k already buys a deployable fleet of 5 robot dogs.)
3. **AI innovation (VLAs):** Vision-Language-Action models are tracking LLM progress with only a slight lag — a single multimodal model digests pixels + plain-language commands and streams torque targets to every joint (no separate see/think/move stack). Industry converging on a **dual-VLA architecture**: a fast intuitive policy for balance/grip + a slow deliberate scene-reasoning model. Figure AI's Helix is the cleanest example — one weight file for every task, already moving sheet metal on BMW pilot lines. NVIDIA dominates compute: Jetson Orin NX at 275 TOPS palm-sized, Thor triples it; GR00T N1 is the first open humanoid-scale VLA, bundled with Isaac Sim pipelines generating billions of synthetic frames (building on OpenVLA-OFT's 25-50× inference-latency cuts and bimanual sub-second hand-eye loops on a single Orin NX). NVIDIA + 1X trained a humanoid to help with dishes in one week. Net: "a VLA can learn from a YouTube video today, fine-tune overnight in Isaac, and clock into a factory in a week."

## Key strategic claim

In their "AI Phase 2" framework, the moat has moved from hard-coded motion trees to **proprietary motion data: whoever owns the fleet logs owns the flywheel**, and the race to gather it is on. Named players: Tesla Optimus, Unitree, Figure, Apptronik, Agility (Digit), 1X/Neo; named tickers in the preview: TXN, RRX, MCHP, ON, IFX, GXO.
