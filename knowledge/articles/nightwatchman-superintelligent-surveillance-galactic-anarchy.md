Forethought proposes that every probe leaving the solar system carry a superintelligent "nightwatchman" ASI that enforces a minimal universal code (respect property rights, no universe-destroying weapons, no astronomical suffering). Because galactic distances make ex post punishment impossible, enforcement must be local and preventive, with a decisive strategic advantage inside each colony.

## Key Takeaways

- Galactic anarchy has three major externalities: unconstrained resource racing ("burning the cosmic commons"), galactic x-risks such as false vacuum decay, and suffering risks.
- Long-distance moral trade and public-goods funding are also hard without credible local enforcement.
- The nightwatchman monitors industrial build-up and new ASIs, blocks probes that lack a copy of itself, and can optionally enforce voluntary contracts and collect taxes for public goods.
- Drawbacks: lock-in of a bad code, a single point of vulnerability if all nightwatchmen share a bug (diversity vs security tradeoff), and extensive surveillance that conflicts with some conceptions of freedom and privacy.
- Mitigations include delaying launches until the code is carefully reflected on, narrowly targeting enforcement to galactic-scale harms, and optionally time-limiting the nightwatchmen.
