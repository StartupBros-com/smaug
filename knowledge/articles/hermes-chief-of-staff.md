Eric Siu presents Hermes as an operational layer rather than a conversational assistant: it retains memory, follows learned playbooks, runs scheduled jobs, and can coordinate specialist agents. The proposed setup moves agent outputs into Slack for team collaboration, Linear for visible work tracking and review, and Obsidian for durable organizational memory. A closed loop of goals, tickets, feedback, and approvals is intended to keep work progressing without constant manual prompting.

The article gives twelve examples of that operating model. They span branded collateral generated from design specifications, multitasked agent workstreams, connected business APIs, content analysis, staged autonomy for email, specialist subagents, subscription-authenticated model access, and remote machine control. The overall recommendation is to start under human review, connect agents to real business context, and expand autonomy only after the system has earned trust.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat the agent as a worker with schedules, memory, and playbooks, not as a chat window opened for isolated requests.
- Use Linear as a project spine: agents create and update tickets, while the operator reviews progress rather than reconstructing it from chat histories.
- Use Obsidian and QMD as external memory so important decisions survive between runs and can compound into reliable automation.
- Connect agents to operational APIs such as CRM, call, content, and analytics systems so their recommendations reflect business data rather than generic assumptions.
- Start outbound work in draft-and-approve mode; increase autonomy to sending only after observing a reliable feedback loop.
- Organize specialist agents around narrow functions and let a manager agent coordinate them against one definition of done.
