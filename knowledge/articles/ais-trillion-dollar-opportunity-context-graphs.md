Jaya Gupta and Ashu Garg argue that agentic software creates a new enterprise data asset: a context graph built from structured decision traces. Rather than merely recording final state in a CRM or ERP, an orchestration layer can preserve the inputs, policies, exceptions, approvals, and precedents that led to a particular action. They contend that this replayable “why” layer can become a new system of record for autonomous work.

## Key Takeaways

- Systems of record preserve canonical objects and state, but often omit the situational reasoning that determines exceptions and approvals.
- A decision trace should record the context gathered across systems, policies evaluated, exception routes, approvers, and resulting state at the time an agent acts.
- Linked decision traces become searchable precedent, allowing organizations to audit, debug, and gradually automate exception-heavy workflows.
- Incumbent operational systems usually see only their own siloed state, while warehouses often receive data after decisions have already occurred; neither naturally captures cross-system decision context at commit time.
- Startups can pursue the opportunity by replacing a legacy system, owning an exception-heavy module while syncing final state back, or creating a new decision-focused system of record.
