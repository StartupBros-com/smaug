Jensen Huang's X essay arguing that **land, power, and shell (LPS)** is now the scarce input for AI factories the way chips, packaging, memory, and networking already were. NVIDIA is applying the same long-term supply-chain discipline it used on semiconductors to lock exceptional LPS sites exclusively for NVIDIA compute. The first deal: partner with SB Energy at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Portsmouth, Ohio; OpenAI is the tenant.

## The deal

- OpenAI will build and operate a full-stack NVIDIA DSX AI factory (GPUs, CPUs, networking, infrastructure software) at PORTS-Pike
- Initial capacity: **4.25 GW**. NVIDIA may extend to the remaining **3.75 GW**
- Each generation of NVIDIA systems on the site is framed as ~**1.5 million GPUs**, or roughly **$150–200B** of NVIDIA revenue; the site can take multiple upgrade cycles over 20 years
- Broader OpenAI commitments through 2030: ~**12 GW** of NVIDIA compute, with a path to ~**16 GW** if PORTS-Pike is extended — Jensen puts that opportunity at ~**$600B** of NVIDIA compute through 2030
- NVIDIA's own guarantee: support for ~4 GW of LPS over a **20-year term**, limited to defined portions of lease and power payments plus a specified residual-value commitment — not the full site cost or all tenant obligations
- Guarantee phases in as data centers come online **2028–2030**; remaining NVIDIA exposure falls as OpenAI pays the lease

## Why NVIDIA is doing this

Most NVIDIA customers (CSPs, investment-grade enterprises) already secure their own LPS. Frontier labs are different: demand for training and inference is growing faster than their balance sheets and long-term credit profiles, so compute — not algorithms or customers — is the constraint. NVIDIA is selectively underwriting exceptional sites where it can host multiple generations of its own stack and serve durable demand. The productive life of the site is the land and power; the GPUs inside can be upgraded repeatedly.

## The circular-financing rebuttal

Jensen's explicit answer to "is this circular financing?" is no: **OpenAI pays the lease**. NVIDIA is using scale and demand visibility to secure the site, the same way it pre-buys wafers. If OpenAI does not use the capacity, NVIDIA compute is treated as fungible — resellable to another qualified CSP, enterprise, lab, or startup. CUDA is the argument for fungibility: a common platform makes installed systems versatile, rentable, and therefore financeable. Most LPS hosting NVIDIA compute will still be secured directly by customers; PORTS-Pike is the selective exception.

## Key Takeaways

- LPS (land, power, shell) is now a first-class NVIDIA supply-chain input, not just a customer problem
- PORTS-Pike is a 20-year exclusive NVIDIA-compute campus, not a one-generation cluster
- The dollar story is upgrade cycles on a long-lived site, not a single GPU shipment
- NVIDIA's guarantee is partial, phased, and declining — designed to answer the circular-financing charge before the market makes it
- CUDA-as-fungibility is the credit argument: if the tenant leaves, the site is still a productive NVIDIA asset
