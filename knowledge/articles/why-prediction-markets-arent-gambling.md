This article argues that prediction markets should be understood as information and coordination systems rather than entertainment gambling. It contrasts sportsbook odds, which it characterizes as being managed to balance a bookmaker's exposure, with tradable probabilities that aggregate participants' views and can guide decisions about projects, policy, and risk. It also highlights reflexivity: market expectations can alter the behavior that helps bring the anticipated outcome about.

## Key Takeaways

- The article distinguishes bookmaker-set odds from prediction-market prices intended to represent a collective estimate of an event's probability.
- It presents internal company forecasting, macroeconomic signals, and geopolitical assessment as potential uses beyond sporting events.
- Market prices can affect decisions by businesses and investors, creating a feedback loop between a forecast and the outcome it describes.
- The author’s core claim is that the useful product of a prediction market is actionable information, not simply the thrill of a wager.
