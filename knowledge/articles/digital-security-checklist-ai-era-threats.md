An X Article that packages a fifteen-step personal digital-security checklist around warnings that increasingly capable AI will amplify phishing, account compromise, and privacy risks. It recommends familiar baseline controls such as unique passwords, hardware keys, disk encryption, and DNS-level tracking protection, alongside choices intended to reduce data exposure. The threat framing is notably alarmist and some claims are oversimplified, so the list is best used as a practical starting point rather than a complete security assessment.

## Key Takeaways

- Use a password manager to generate unique credentials, and save randomized answers to account-recovery questions alongside them.
- Prefer hardware security keys for important accounts, keep recovery keys in separate locations, and enable device biometrics where appropriate.
- Protect data at rest with FileVault or BitLocker, and reduce the attack surface created by unnecessary internet-connected home devices.
- Limit tracking and network exposure with privacy-focused messaging, a privacy-oriented browser and search engine, virtual card numbers, a virtual mailing address, and DNS-level blocking.
- Treat unsolicited email links as suspicious, use a VPN selectively on untrusted networks, and monitor unexpected outbound network connections from installed apps.
