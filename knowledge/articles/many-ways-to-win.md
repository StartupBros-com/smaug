Steph Ango's 2023 catalog of **eighty** competitive strategies (he later called it "70" on X) for how organisms, people, products, organizations, and networks accumulate energy and survive. The thesis is not "find the one true moat" — successful things optimize a **narrow, often unusual combination** of vectors. Each strategy is given a one-line definition plus biology and business examples, grouped into thirteen categories. Posted as a reply to "what is your moat if everything can just be generated": these edges still apply when generation itself is free.

## Categories

- **Accumulation** — usership/network effects, completeness, aggregation, diversification, omnipresence, cloning (Visa, Walmart, aspen groves, franchises).
- **Price** — affordability, luxury/Veblen, skimming tiny transactions (credit cards, baleen whales), bundling (Office, seeds in fruit).
- **Time** — heritage, craftsmanship, organic growth that cannot be rushed, endurance.
- **Uniqueness** — specialization vs versatility vs hybrid, divergence, authenticity, rarity vs artificial scarcity, secrecy, irreverence.
- **Offense** — violence, litigation, nettlesomeness, sabotage, parasitism, scavenging, espionage, swarming, highest bidder, chokepoints, puppeteering.
- **Defense** — deterrence, reliability, predictability vs unpredictability, decentralization, security, privacy, durability, neutrality, obscurity, antifragility.
- **Deception** — camouflage, mimicry, decoy, lure, infiltration.
- **Timing** — first-mover, second-mover/fast-follower, last-mover (Apple waiting out failed approaches).
- **Accreditation** — monopoly/regulatory capture, prestige, curation.
- **Collaboration** — union, alliance, emergence, centralization, standardization, symbiosis, herding, distributed ownership, transparency.
- **Speed and scale** — iteration, efficiency, agility, precision, blitzing, composability, modularity.
- **Ease** — intuitiveness, fun, simplicity, low-friction, charm.
- **Transformation** — malleability, metamorphosis, copycat.

## Key Takeaways

- A moat is a **stack**, not a slogan. Winners pick a few of these and combine them in a way competitors do not.
- Several of the strongest entries survive AI commoditization of output: craftsmanship, authenticity, secrecy, last-mover timing, curation, charm, usership, chokepoints, and antifragility do not require being the only one who can generate text or pixels.
- Biology and business are treated as the same game — dandelions sit next to Coca-Cola, cordyceps next to puppeteering, muscles next to BitTorrent — which makes the list a scanning tool rather than a Porter rewrite.
