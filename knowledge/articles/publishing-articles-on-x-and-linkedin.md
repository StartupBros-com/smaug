Jake Ward's X article argues against the claim that long-form publishing is obsolete, with the available preview saying that articles are making a substantial return despite the rise of short-form posts and threads. The bookmark data does not include the article body, so this entry is limited to its title, preview, and the framing supplied by the quoting post.

Rob Hoffman uses the article to make a stricter content-strategy argument: search engines, LLMs, and social platforms do not reward mass-produced summaries that add no new information. He frames "Authority Content" as original information, perspective, or data, and advocates distributing fewer, stronger pieces rather than flooding channels with generated articles.

## Key Takeaways

- Long-form articles remain a distribution format worth publishing on X and LinkedIn.
- Content needs genuinely distinct information, perspective, or data to earn authority.
- Generative tools can scale output, but they do not make derivative content competitive with the original source.
- The suggested operating principle is to publish less frequently while improving substance and distribution.
