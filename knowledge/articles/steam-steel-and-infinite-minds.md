Ivan Zhao compares AI to the foundational materials that reshaped earlier economies: steel enabled taller and lighter buildings, while steam freed factories from rivers and made new forms of industrial organization possible. He argues that AI offers knowledge work a comparable supply of "infinite minds," but organizations will not realize its potential by merely attaching chatbots to their existing human-centered processes.

At the individual level, Zhao sees software engineers already moving from doing work directly to orchestrating several coding agents at once. Extending that leverage to other knowledge workers depends on solving two constraints: fragmented context across communication and business tools, and a way to verify the quality of work outside domains such as code where tests provide immediate feedback.

For organizations, he proposes that agents can make human communication less of a load-bearing coordination mechanism by carrying context across workflows, handling repetitive requests, and surfacing decisions for brief human review. Zhao describes Notion's early use of more than 700 agents alongside its approximately 1,000 employees for tasks including notes, knowledge synthesis, IT requests, feedback logging, onboarding, and status reports.

Zhao's economic analogy is a shift from human-scaled Florences to AI-enabled Tokyos: larger, faster, and initially less legible systems that support many more combinations of people and work. His conclusion is that the important design question is not how AI can act as a copilot inside current workflows, but how companies should be rebuilt once continuously operating agents remove their old coordination limits.

## Key Takeaways

- AI's largest organizational effect may come from redesigned workflows, not isolated chat interfaces added to existing tools.
- Broad knowledge-work automation needs consolidated context and credible ways to assess or verify outputs.
- Humans can gain leverage by supervising agent loops at decision points instead of performing every intermediate review.
- AI agents can reduce routine coordination work, but the resulting organizations may require new rhythms and management models.
- Historical technology shifts suggest the strongest productivity gains arrive after institutions are reorganized around the new capability.
