Leon Lin argues that AI should accelerate execution rather than substitute for design judgment. The designer remains responsible for the product's purpose, audience, emotional intent, creative direction, and taste; AI tools such as Cursor, Codex, and Claude translate that intentional context into components and implementation work.

The article presents three levels of AI-assisted design. Design skills offer a fast way to avoid obvious visual mistakes; a component-by-component workflow offers more control and craft; and a curated inspiration board provides a middle path. Across all three, the recommended process is to establish meaning and branding first, study and organize references, map structure and content, then iterate on bounded components before refining assets and interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- Decide who the product is for, what it solves, and how it should feel before asking AI to generate a UI.
- Build taste through deliberate reference collection and analysis of layout, spacing, typography, structure, and interaction.
- Use AI for constrained, component-level tasks instead of a single request to generate an entire product.
- Design skills are quick and useful for baseline quality, but intentionally directed workflows can produce more distinctive work.
- Custom assets, layering, motion, and final refinement create cohesion that generic generated designs often lack.
