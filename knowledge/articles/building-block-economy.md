Mitchell Hashimoto argues that agentic software production changes the economics of software toward reusable, well-documented building blocks. Rather than concentrating every feature in a polished mainline application, maintainers can expose components that people and agents combine into specialized tools, forks, and experiments.

He contrasts Ghostty's update-check scale with libghostty's rapid integration into other products, and describes lower quality thresholds, broader niche awareness, reduced feature-request pressure, and externally generated R&D as benefits of the model. The essay also identifies a commercial challenge: agents currently tend to select open and free components over closed alternatives, even as high-quality mainline products retain a role.

## Key Takeaways

- Agents are particularly effective at composing proven components, increasing demand for reusable primitives and clear documentation.
- Building-block ecosystems support niche experimentation while letting maintainers selectively incorporate working ideas into the mainline product.
- Open software may gain a distribution advantage in agent-mediated selection, leaving commercialization an unresolved strategic problem.
