Vitalik Buterin argues that an era of stronger governments, corporations, and networked social movements requires deliberate counterweights. His central concern is that technology erodes the geographic and organizational limits that once constrained large institutions, making concentrated power more capable of coercion, extraction, and mass coordination.

## Key Takeaways

- State, commercial, and social power can deliver useful services, but each becomes dangerous when it lacks effective constraints.
- Governments should primarily provide predictable rules: the article's shorthand is that government should behave like a game rather than a player.
- Divided branches, local autonomy, competition among countries, and meaningful exit options can reduce domination by a centralized state.
- Firms operating under profit and competitive pressure can diverge from their users' interests, especially as scale lets them shape markets, politics, and culture.
- The answer is not one institution replacing another, but a durable distribution of power in which major forces check each other.
