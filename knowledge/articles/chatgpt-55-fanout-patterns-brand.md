Wil Reynolds and Nick Haigler argue that ChatGPT 5.5 changed the GEO game by injecting company and personal brand names into fan-out (sub-query) searches, which locks the eventual answer to those brands and starves listicle farms. The new KPI is not "am I cited in the answer" but "is my brand inside the fan-out itself." When the model searches "Lily Ray GEO strategy" or "Seer Interactive GEO research" instead of "top GEO agencies," only the named brand can reasonably win.

They held the line against scaled listicles for two years on the bet that answer engines would eventually punish ballot-box stuffing the way Google punished BMW and JCPenney. ChatGPT 5.5 is that reckoning: fan-outs that used to mash up "best SEO agency" + "best PPC agency" lists now look like "iPullRank AI search study" and "Lily Ray 2025 GEO penalty." Being in the fan-out means you *are* the result, not a candidate in it.

On a 617-prompt set aimed at VPs of growth / CMOs, plus 30 repeats of a "which agencies are leading the AI-search conversation" prompt, personal brands (Lily Ray, Mike King, Wil Reynolds) show up as the query itself in a large share of runs. Co-citation in the final answer is downstream of those branded fan-outs. They also flag `site:`-restricted fan-outs as a trust signal — the model is doing what humans do when they type a known brand plus the question.

The practical audit is a "do humans care" split: bucket every URL's traffic into algorithmic (ChatGPT, Google, Perplexity) vs human (trade-journal referrals, LinkedIn, Teams, direct), then ask what share of the next ten pieces can win hearts and minds. Bing Webmaster Tools now exposes grounding / fan-out queries. Reynolds' accompanying tweet adds a monitoring stack: AlertMouse on trade pubs and category brands (Search Engine Journal, Search Engine Land, eMarketer, Ahrefs, Semrush) plus PersonalBrander harvests of whether Lily Ray, Mike King, Rand Fishkin, etc. ever mention your trademarks.

## Key Takeaways

- ChatGPT 5.5 fan-outs shifted from generic "best X" listicle bait to branded queries that lock the answer to the named person or company
- Track a new KPI: brand visibility *inside* fan-outs, not just citation share in the final answer
- Human-vetted distribution (trade pubs, LinkedIn, personal brands, conferences) is what hardwires a brand into the model; scaled chunking and listicles do not
- `site:` fan-outs make owned-domain authority harder to shortcut — the model is looking for content authored on *your* domain
- Brand co-citation with category-defining people (Lily Ray, Mike King) is a leading indicator; run the same prompt monthly and watch who pulls away
- Gemini 3 fan-outs previously showed ~0.1% overlap run-to-run; ChatGPT is similarly noisy at n=1, so stack repeats before you trust a single trace
