Muninn reconstructed 900+ long trades Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie) took between 2019 and 2022 and treated every beginner entry question as a priced dial rather than a rule. The tweet that recirculated the piece is the punchline of that study: once you measure extension in ADR, *when* you click matters less than *how far the stock has already run*. Buying too early — under a quarter ADR off the low — is the expensive mistake; the stop sits inside the noise and ~91% of those trades die on the entry day.

The rest of the article is a set of tradeoff tables on opening-range window, extension, order type, scaling, and re-buys, plus nine process changes Muninn made after seeing the numbers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Opening range is a dial, not a secret.** Simulated 1 / 5 / 15 / 30-minute ORB highs on the same names, stop at the then-low of day, exit first close under the 10-day: 1-minute prints 24.8% win rate / 3.61R mean; 30-minute prints 36.9% / 1.54R. Waiting buys comfort and win rate; the tighter 1-minute stop is why mean R is ~2× higher.
- **The bill is worse fills, not missed trades.** Setups the slower window never triggers actually *lose* money at the faster entry (−30R / −78R / −84R). Across 805 setups the 1-minute harvest is 2865R vs 976R at 30 minutes — 1889R (66%) given up, almost entirely from a higher price and wider stop on trades you still take.
- **Episodic pivots hate the fast window.** EP win rate goes 29.7% → 49.0% from 1-minute to 30-minute; breakouts only 24.4% → 35.8%. A gap needs room. Do not run one entry recipe on both setups.
- **Extension has a hard cheap end.** Qullamaggie's stated band (prefer ⅓–⅔ ADR, skip above 1 ADR) matches behavior: median entry 0.47 ADR, 87% under 1 ADR. Under 0.25 ADR: 8.9% win rate, **−0.47R**. Over 1.00 ADR: +2.63R and 23% average gain. Above his line the data does not punish you, but you are on your own judgement. If it feels like a chase, size down rather than skip — the strongest names often never give a clean tag.
- **Tail-heavy to the point of parody.** In the 0.25–0.50 ADR bucket the best 5% of trades produce 99% of total R. Fewer than four in ten finish green at any window; the median trade is a full stop-out; median hold is 1–3 days. Qullamaggie once stacked 31 losers in a row. Expectancy is the tail, not the typical trade.
- **A click is not the entry.** Up to one in four entries is a scaled-in position (EPs twice as often as breakouts). About one in eight same-day entries is a second attempt after a stop. A stop is the cost of one attempt, not a verdict on the idea.
- **Order type follows account size.** If you have to ask, use market. He does use buy-stops (CODX filled 60k during an 11%-in-ten-seconds halt). Later limit-order preference is slippage from size, not a beginner edge. He has never used a hotkey.
- **Moving the stop to breakeven is comfort, not edge.** Tests of day-3 / day-5 / day-10 BE versus holding the original stop did not improve expectancy.
- **The trigger is not the edge.** Opening-range high + stop under the day low is independently used by Dan Zanger, Jeff Sun, and much of the Traderlion roster. Two of them refuse a single entry-time almost word for word. @jfsrev's 8-year hard rule is the opposite: wait 30 minutes. Same data, opposite conclusion — the work is tuning the dials so they fit *your* account and wiring.
