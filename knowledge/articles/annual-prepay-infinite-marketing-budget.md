Jason Cohen describes how annual billing can ease a SaaS company's growth constraint: acquisition costs are paid immediately, while monthly revenue normally arrives too slowly to replenish the cash. When a customer's annual payment, less the cash needed to serve that customer, covers acquisition cost before the advertising charge is due, the cash cycle can fund further marketing without external capital.

The article argues that this only works when payback is measured with gross profit rather than revenue. It pairs annual-plan design with levers that improve unit economics—lower CAC, stronger pricing and positioning, and higher gross margin—and cautions founders to account for churn and clear refund or cancellation policies.

## Key Takeaways

- Calculate acquisition payback against monthly recurring revenue multiplied by gross margin, not revenue alone.
- Fast growth can worsen a bootstrapped company's cash position because acquisition spending precedes monthly collections.
- Encourage annual prepayment by making month-to-month plans more expensive while preserving a monthly option for customers who need it.
- Reserve the amount required to deliver the annual commitment; only the remaining upfront cash is available to cover acquisition.
- Treat retention and cancellation terms as part of the model rather than assuming annual plans eliminate churn.
