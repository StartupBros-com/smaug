This X Article recounts a $TRUMP memecoin trade that the author hedged with prediction-market shares tied to the possibility that the launch was a hack. The position was designed so the prediction-market payout could cover most of the meme-coin loss in the adverse scenario, while a legitimate launch left the author exposed to the coin's upside. It uses the anecdote to argue that prediction markets enable unusually specific, event-linked hedges for headline, policy, or operational risks.

## Key Takeaways

- The author paired a directional spot purchase with contracts intended to pay out if the central event proved fraudulent.
- The hedge limited losses in the scenario the author initially considered most likely, while preserving potential gains if the launch was genuine.
- Unlike broad index hedges, prediction-market contracts can target a narrowly defined event such as a hack, election, or policy decision.
- The account is a single speculative trade narrative, but its core idea is that event markets can be used for risk transfer as well as outright forecasting.
