Shane Parrish explains second-order thinking as a decision discipline that looks past an action's immediate result to the consequences that follow when incentives change and other people react. The framework is intended to improve strategic choices by extending the time horizon and treating downstream effects as part of the initial decision.

## Key Takeaways

- Ask not only what happens immediately, but what is likely to happen next and after that.
- Evaluate outcomes across several horizons, from minutes and days to months and years.
- Record first-, second-, and third-order effects explicitly in a decision template rather than relying on intuition alone.
- Review earlier predictions after outcomes arrive to improve future judgment.
- In business decisions, account for likely responses from employees, competitors, suppliers, customers, and regulators.
- An option with an unattractive immediate result can still be superior if its later effects are beneficial.
