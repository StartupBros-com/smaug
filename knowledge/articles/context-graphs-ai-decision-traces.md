Jaya Gupta and Ashu Garg argue that AI agents need more than access to systems of record and governance rules: they need durable records of how decisions were actually made. They call the accumulated, queryable record of inputs, policies, exceptions, approvals, and outcomes a context graph. Capturing those traces in the orchestration path can make precedent searchable, enable audits and debugging, and give companies a system of record for decisions rather than only for current object state.

The article frames this as an opportunity for agent-native startups. Incumbent operational systems tend to preserve current state and are siloed; warehouses observe data after the decision has occurred. An agent system operating across the workflow can instead record cross-system context at the moment an action is taken, then build a compounding history of human-in-the-loop decisions that supports progressively more automation.

## Key Takeaways

- Rules describe what generally should happen, while decision traces preserve the case-specific exceptions, approvals, and precedents that explain what did happen.
- A context graph connects business entities and agent runs through decision events, making the rationale for autonomy queryable and replayable.
- The strongest initial opportunities are exception-heavy workflows and cross-functional "glue" roles, where organizational knowledge currently lives in conversations rather than durable systems.
- Agent orchestration layers can capture decision context at commit time, unlike incumbent systems focused on current state or warehouses populated after the fact.
