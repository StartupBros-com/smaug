This article presents an "Autonomous Build Manager Doctrine": a reusable working-style prompt for coding agents assigned to multi-hour or longer projects. It aims to supplement a task brief with a process for establishing the goal, understanding its motivation, gathering context, planning work, executing iteratively, documenting status, and verifying the result against an explicit desired outcome.

The doctrine is deliberately expansive, asking the agent to act with the judgment and quality bar of a senior technical lead rather than merely complete a narrow instruction. It also draws a boundary around its intended use: routine, tightly prescribed, and short tasks should use lighter-weight procedures instead of the full framework.

## Key Takeaways

- The prompt separates the task’s requested outcome from the operating discipline needed to carry out substantial autonomous work.
- It requires the agent to restate the task and identify concrete pain points, beneficiaries, timing, and a "dream state" before implementation.
- Planning guidance calls for phased milestones, acceptance criteria, technology choices, risks, escalation conditions, and time or budget bounds.
- Execution guidance emphasizes reading relevant context, testing and reviewing continuously, refining the plan when evidence changes, and recording progress in an append-only status log.
- Completion is evaluated against the stated outcome and quality checks, not only whether the literal task specification was addressed.
