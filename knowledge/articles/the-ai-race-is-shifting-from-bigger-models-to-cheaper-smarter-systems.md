CNBC's article describes a shift in AI model selection away from raw leaderboard capability and toward task fit, cost, and operational control. Gavin Baker builds on that observation with an investment thesis: cheaper, more token-efficient open or closed models could improve customers' intelligence-per-dollar and expand total token demand.

In that scenario, declining inference margins at leading model providers would reallocate more of the industry's economic value to infrastructure vendors that can serve tokens at the lowest cost. Baker notes that the change is not yet complete: lower-cost models may account for substantial volume, but the most capable models still capture most economic value.

## Key Takeaways

- Model buyers are increasingly balancing quality against task requirements, price, and control rather than selecting only by frontier rankings.
- Lower model costs can improve return on AI spending and potentially increase demand for inference tokens.
- If high-margin frontier inference gives way to lower-cost alternatives, infrastructure providers could capture a greater share of profit dollars.
- The prospective model-layer winners would pair adequate capability with superior token efficiency.
- The redistribution remains a thesis rather than a settled market outcome because the most intelligent models still retain outsized value.
