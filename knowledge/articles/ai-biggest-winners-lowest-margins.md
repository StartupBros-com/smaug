Daniel Kornum argues that AI may create its greatest earnings impact in low-margin, labor-intensive industries such as manufacturing, logistics, distribution, staffing, and field services. In these businesses, modest reductions in operating cost can transform profits because the baseline margin is already thin.

The central opportunity is not simply replacing frontline labor or giving employees another AI interface. It is reducing the coordination layer around human work: scheduling, dispatch, approvals, exception handling, customer updates, claims, invoicing, and reconciliation. The article contends that AI agents should operate across the systems workers already use, escalating only cases that need judgment and improving from approval feedback.

## Key Takeaways

- A small cost reduction has disproportionate profit impact for businesses operating on single-digit margins.
- The hidden coordination work surrounding frontline labor is a significant, repeatable target for AI automation.
- AI deployments are more likely to realize value when embedded in existing systems and workflows than when they require every employee to adopt a new tool.
- Early adopters can establish a lower cost base before efficiency gains spread through a commoditized market and are competed away.
