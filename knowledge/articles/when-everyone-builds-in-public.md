Hiten Shah argues that building in public lost much of its early value as it became a polished, measurable content practice. Early founders shared uncertain decisions and unfinished work to test conviction and find collaborators; today, branded screenshots, rehearsed reflections, AI-written posts, SEO-shaped headlines, and scheduling tools can simulate authenticity without the underlying risk.

The article’s prescription is not to post more. As public visibility becomes universal—even among CEOs—the differentiator is sharing details another person could not credibly have produced: lived experience, real trade-offs, and decisions whose outcomes remain uncertain. Building in public is most useful as a way to learn, refine thinking, find alignment, and create trust around work that matters.

## Key Takeaways

- Visibility no longer differentiates builders when everyone can publish continuously and optimize their public presence.
- The trust-building element of early public work came from its risk, uncertainty, and exposure of genuine blind spots.
- AI and publishing tools make polished, apparently authentic communication easy to reproduce, raising the value of specific lived details.
- Builders should use sharing to improve their thinking and discover aligned collaborators, rather than treating it chiefly as a personal-brand channel.
