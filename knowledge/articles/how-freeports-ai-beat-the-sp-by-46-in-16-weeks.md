Freeport Markets presents a retrospective backtest of an AI system that ingested social and news content, ranked trading signals, and held the highest-confidence directional recommendations for seven days. It reports a 54.1% net return versus 7.6% for SPY between January 27 and May 14, 2026, but emphasizes that the apparent edge was episodic: much of the result came from correlated bets on the Iran-driven oil move and the AI-capex semiconductor cycle. The article frames the product's thesis as exploiting gradual information diffusion from credible sources while acknowledging that this sample cannot establish durable, independent alpha.

## Key Takeaways

- The reported test processed 68,004 inputs, surfaced 5,237 candidate trades, and delivered 803 notifications; the raw volume should not be mistaken for 803 independent observations.
- The largest drivers were long energy exposure during an Iran-related supply narrative and concentrated semiconductor exposure to names such as Intel, Marvell, Micron, and AMD during the AI-capex cycle.
- The strategy's claimed advantage is episodic responsiveness to news-heavy macro moments rather than a uniform daily alpha stream.
- Freeport argues that credible-source curation, lower-liquidity venues, willingness to act before broad confirmation, and gradual attention diffusion can preserve an informational edge.
- The author explicitly warns that the backtest is not investment advice, that returns may be period-specific, and that the associated crypto and derivatives venues carry material loss, liquidity, regulatory, and counterparty risks.
