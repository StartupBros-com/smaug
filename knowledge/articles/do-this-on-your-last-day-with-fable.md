This playbook argues that a short window of access to a frontier model should be used to extract durable judgment rather than create disposable demonstrations. Its central test is whether a cheaper model could reproduce the result later: if it can, the task is lower priority than artifacts that preserve the stronger model’s reasoning.

It proposes five ways to bank that judgment: have the model write project standards and skills; conduct a business audit with an executable roadmap; turn research into linked, atomic notes; run a small number of bounded, verifiable autonomous goals; and record the approach used for every non-trivial solved problem. The resulting documents, notes, and skills can continue guiding less capable models after the original model is unavailable or repriced.

## Key Takeaways

- Use a frontier model to author checkable standards and decision criteria that a less capable model can consistently apply.
- Preserve strategic reasoning as ranked roadmaps and atomized research notes instead of relying on one-off chat outputs.
- Define hard success conditions and turn caps for unattended work so autonomous runs are both verifiable and bounded.
- Capture the reasoning behind difficult solutions as a reusable project asset, not merely the final patch.
