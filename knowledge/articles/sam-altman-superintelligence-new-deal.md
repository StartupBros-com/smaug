An Axios report framed Sam Altman's view of approaching superintelligence as a public-policy transition on the scale of the Progressive Era and the New Deal. As summarized in the bookmarked post, Altman argues that the United States needs a new social contract to address fast-moving AI capabilities, labor displacement, cyber threats, and wider social disruption.

The article body was unavailable in the bookmark data because Axios returned a Cloudflare challenge, so this note records only the claims attributed to the report in the bookmark and its quoted post. Those claims include Altman's warning that soon-to-be-released models could make a world-shaking cyberattack possible within the year, alongside concern about job losses and systems people may not be able to control.

## Key Takeaways

- The reported policy framing compares the potential AI transition to earlier periods of large-scale economic and social reform.
- Altman reportedly calls for a new social contract rather than treating advanced AI solely as a product or technical-policy issue.
- Cybersecurity is presented as an immediate concern: the report attributes to Altman the possibility of major AI-enabled cyberattacks within a year.
- The bookmark also attributes warnings about widespread job loss, social upheaval, and loss of human control over machines to Altman.
