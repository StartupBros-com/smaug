This community survey groups 276 reported Hermes Agent deployments into 16 categories and argues that persistent, tool-using agents are becoming operational infrastructure rather than conversational interfaces. The examples span development, integrations, personal assistance, business operations, content production, research, and enterprise infrastructure, with the same runtime used to retain context, trigger work, and coordinate external systems over time.

The article's central distinction is persistence: an agent that can remember workflows, observe systems, schedule actions, and delegate tasks can operate as a continuing service instead of responding only within a chat session. Its examples include multi-agent coding and monitoring setups, family assistants, sales and content pipelines, research ingestion, and low-cost self-hosted deployments.

## Key Takeaways

- The survey frames Hermes adoption as a broad set of real deployments rather than a single agent use case.
- Development and integrations are the largest cited areas, including code quality bots, orchestration, messaging, and cloud tools.
- Persistent memory, scheduling, observation, and tool coordination are presented as the capabilities that turn an LLM into infrastructure.
- The reported implementations range from inexpensive personal systems to enterprise and multi-agent deployments.
