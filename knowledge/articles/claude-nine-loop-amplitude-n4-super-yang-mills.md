Anthropic Science Blog post about Claude computing a nine-loop scattering amplitude in planar N=4 super-Yang-Mills, beyond the previous eight-loop record set by Lance Dixon and collaborators. Only the title and the announcing tweet were captured (the page body did not extract), so details below come from the tweet.

## Key Takeaways

- Scattering amplitudes are computed in layers of "loops"; each extra loop adds precision but costs exponentially more compute, and most calculations stop at two or three loops.
- Physicist and writer @4gravitons challenged an AI to pass eight loops within an academic-scale compute budget.
- Given a single prompt, Claude ran largely unsupervised for days in Claude Science, using methods developed by Dixon and colleagues, at a total cost of a few thousand dollars.
- Dixon independently verified the result.
