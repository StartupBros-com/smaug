Arvind Narayanan’s ICML 2026 keynote, presented as annotated slides with an edited transcript, asks how people should adapt as AI capabilities advance. It presents the AI-as-normal-technology framework as the appropriate lens for AI’s impacts unless a future discontinuity, such as recursive self-improvement, changes the underlying situation.

The talk distinguishes taking recursive self-improvement seriously from assuming that one laboratory achievement would abruptly make human work obsolete. Instead, it argues that jobs will change substantially and require extensive adaptation. Narayanan closes by considering a human–AI “co-superintelligence” rather than treating AI progress only as a displacement story.

## Key Takeaways

- AI should be assessed as a transformative but still normal technology unless a genuine discontinuity emerges
- Recursive self-improvement deserves scrutiny, but no single lab milestone necessarily ends human employment
- The future of work will demand substantial adaptation rather than passive acceptance of technological change
- Human–AI co-superintelligence is offered as a constructive model for the resulting division of labor
