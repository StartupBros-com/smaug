An opinionated case for packaging on-premise LLM deployments as a consulting and managed-support business for clients with sensitive data. The article contrasts recurring cloud-GPU costs with locally hosted AMD Strix Halo systems, arguing that data residency can be a more compelling sales proposition than lower inference cost. Its revenue and adoption figures are presented as the author's claims, not independently verified results.

## Key Takeaways

- Unified-memory Strix Halo systems can make 70B-class open models more practical locally than consumer GPUs with limited dedicated VRAM, though throughput and ROCm compatibility remain constraints.
- The suggested deployment pairs Linux, Ollama, and Open WebUI, then uses RAG to make a private document assistant useful for a client's internal corpus.
- The commercial pitch centers on physical control of regulated or NDA-bound data; setup, support, fine-tuning, and custom-agent work are proposed as service packages.
- Local hosting is positioned for privacy-sensitive, high-volume workloads rather than as a full replacement for frontier models or high-concurrency cloud serving.
