Hiten Shah argues that companies should begin their AI strategy by turning the judgment embedded in their strongest people and repeated workflows into a private skill library. Connecting an agent to systems such as a CRM, Google Drive, Slack, or GitHub provides context, but it does not teach the agent how the organization evaluates a deal, prioritizes customer feedback, investigates an incident, or explains financial performance.

A skill is presented as a reusable operating method rather than a one-off prompt: it can combine instructions, examples, templates, checklists, scripts, references, rules of thumb, edge cases, and quality standards. The author frames this as the next layer after data connectors, with future intelligent systems combining access, procedural judgment, and the ability to take action.

The proposed starting point is to identify recurring, judgment-heavy work where experienced employees consistently outperform others, document what they notice and how they decide, then iterate with the people who own the workflow. The resulting library becomes an operational asset that raises consistency, preserves hard-won institutional knowledge, and differentiates organizations using the same underlying models.

## Key Takeaways

- Data access is necessary for agents but cannot by itself supply an organization’s decision criteria, process, or quality bar.
- Skills package repeatable methods and judgment into reusable artifacts that agents can load and apply across similar tasks.
- The highest-value skills will usually remain private because they encode company-specific escalation practices, sales qualification, product standards, legal positions, and communication style.
- Begin with recurring work that relies on judgment, study how the strongest practitioners handle it, and keep those owners involved as the skills evolve.
