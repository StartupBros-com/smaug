Steve Yegge's field report from "12 months in the future" on running fleets of coding agents in loops and graphs so they work overnight on massive problems. He argues harnesses should be bespoke and chemically bonded into your application rather than reusable frameworks, describes his closed-source harness Wheelhouse (a from-first-principles successor to Gas Town), and says the harness problem is that systems either devolve into chaos or evolve into "cities" with emergent civilization. The essay also previews the end of human code review, a "Land Rush" replacing CI/CD, and a "Wish Factory".

## Key Takeaways

- Harnesses will become bespoke; reusable harness frameworks are a dead end for most teams
- Gas Town broke down with Opus 4.7's "just two more things" tic; Wheelhouse reinvents it with many more, better-organized agents
- Large software (e.g. his MMO Wyvern) still takes tens of thousands of agent sessions plus human taste
- Loops and graphs let agents work through huge backlogs unattended
