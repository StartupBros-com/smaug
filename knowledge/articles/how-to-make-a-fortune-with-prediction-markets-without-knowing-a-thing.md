This article outlines four ways the author approaches prediction-market trading: comparing prices across venues, hedging live sporting events with limit orders, earning platform liquidity incentives, and using specialized analytics or copy-trading tools. It frames prediction markets as a renewed opportunity for betting-arbitrage techniques, while the approaches still depend on execution quality, available liquidity, and exposure to changing prices.

## Key Takeaways

- Cross-market arbitrage seeks to lock in a price difference by allocating stakes across equivalent or related contracts on different platforms.
- The author describes hedging a pre-game sports wager with a later opposing order as odds move during play, ideally using limit orders.
- Providing two-sided limit-order liquidity can earn rewards on eligible Polymarket markets, but filled orders leave the provider exposed to the underlying outcome.
- Market-specific scanners, analytics, and trading tools can help surface opportunities, although they do not remove trading or platform risk.
