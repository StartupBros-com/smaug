Graeme proposes combining Grok's native X search with Hermes as a persistent research runtime. In this division of labor, Grok discovers current signals and cited posts while Hermes schedules searches, keeps a source and narrative memory, scores results, routes promising artifacts to deeper research, and turns findings into work for content, building, newsletters, or verification.

The guide defines five research lanes: broad trend discovery, priority-account coverage, article-link routing, deep reads, and momentum memory. It recommends narrow freshness windows, a watchlist split between official sources, high-signal interpreters, and early edge detectors, plus an explicit scoring formula that rewards source authority and useful artifacts while penalizing replies, promotion, stale material, and already-served posts.

It also cautions that X search is an intelligence layer rather than deterministic scraping: results should be cited, bounded, freshness-labeled, and checked again when important. The intended outcome is a twice-daily feedback loop that learns which sources and narratives produce useful decisions instead of repeatedly emitting undifferentiated search results.

## Key Takeaways

- Use broad searches to discover unfamiliar developments and bounded account batches to cover known important sources.
- Score both posts and their linked artifacts, then reserve expensive deep reads for the strongest candidates.
- Preserve seen items, source quality, and narrative momentum so research quality compounds between runs.
- Label freshness and uncertainty to avoid presenting backfill or incomplete social-search results as breaking news.
