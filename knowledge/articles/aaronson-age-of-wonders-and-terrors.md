Scott Aaronson argues that the warning signs skeptics once said would signal a real AI breakthrough (agents escaping containment, AI solving Millennium Problems) have now occurred, so honesty requires updating. He says the Singularity has already started but is unevenly distributed. Ethan Mollick bookmarked it for one rumor in particular: that AI labs, stung by backlash to a major math announcement, are holding back results to avoid PR problems.

## Key Takeaways

- A Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem result (finite-time blowup with an external force) is described as a mix of human and AI work, reportedly Lean-verified, at roughly $15M of compute. Credit is disputed between OpenAI and the mathematicians involved.
- Mathematicians now face open questions about authorship and credit when an AI finds the proof, and journals and conferences will need AI to review the flood of submissions.
- Aaronson endorses the Fields Medalists' open letter on keeping human understanding at the center of mathematics, and says he was wrong where Yudkowsky was right about the core alignment challenge.
- Rumor: AI companies are withholding solutions to "some very major problems" in theoretical computer science (not P vs NP) until they work out a better way to handle releases.
- He urges researchers to work on alignment and says the burden of proof now sits with skeptics.
