This long-form prompt proposes using local AI-agent session archives as behavioral evidence rather than treating them as a source of generic self-help. It sets out six gated phases: inventory the archives, sample them within explicit limits, collect dated evidence, test hypotheses through an interview, turn validated patterns into a work analysis, and leave reusable planning artifacts.

Its practical value is the operational discipline around a potentially intrusive exercise: do not read logs before the user approves a mining plan, limit sampling rather than loading whole transcripts, require three dated occurrences before treating a behavior as a pattern, and show diffs before changing agent configuration. The promised outputs are an evidence file, a six-month mirror, a 30-day roadmap, and proposed automations that reduce repeated requests.

## Key Takeaways

- Survey local agent-history sources and obtain approval for a focused sampling plan before reading their contents.
- Cap reads and use aggregate pattern searches, periodically writing dated receipts into an evidence file.
- Separate evidence collection from interpretation, and test conclusions with the user before presenting a behavioral assessment.
- Convert validated findings into concrete delegation, focus, and scheduling decisions, while requiring approval for every configuration change.
