Din (EagleRev) treats a freshly funded startup as a time-boxed buyer: cash that must be deployed and a founder who can approve spend the same week. The article lists ten public directories that name those companies, most of them free, and argues the edge is arriving in the first weeks after a batch, round, or launch — not scraping Crunchbase once and emailing last year's raise.

## Accelerator and startup directories

1. **Y Combinator directory** — public list of every YC company, filterable by batch, industry, region, and status. Recent batches are the high-intent slice: they just raised and were told to grow immediately. Pull the company, the batch as a funding timestamp, the one-line description, and founder names.
2. **Techstars portfolio** — same structure across a different accelerator, useful when a regional program maps onto a specific market.
3. **Wellfound** — startups listed next to the roles they are hiring for. An open role is a signal of which function is under strain and where budget already went.
4. **Product Hunt** — every launch is a company that just shipped and now needs users. The launch date is a public timestamp on the window where a founder is most receptive to traffic or sales help.

## Funding databases

5. **Crunchbase** — broadest coverage of rounds. Filter to the last 90 days for fresh cash plus a mandate; the investor field is a pointer into the portfolio pages below.
6. **PitchBook public profiles** — deal data (who raised, who invested, valuation) on indexed public pages, usable without a subscription for larger rounds.
7. **Dealroom** — European startup and funding coverage that US-centric tools miss (a funded German or Nordic company often appears nowhere else).
8. **Angel and VC portfolio pages** — called the most underused source. Every fund publishes its book on its own site; the fund already did the diligence, and the page is free.

## Filings and regional directories

9. **SEC EDGAR** — Form D filings record private raises with amount and company officers. The risk-factors section in a full filing is the company stating, in its own words, what it is worried about.
10. **EU Startups and other regional directories** — country-level lists of funded companies outside the crowded US sets, plus the language and market context needed to open a message properly.

## How to turn the lists into a pipeline

Capture on every record: company name and domain, the funding timestamp (batch, round date, or launch date), a one-line description in their words, founder/exec names, and team size. Qualify with a model against your ICP before sending, with a reason on every drop. Run the sources on a schedule — new batches, rounds, and launches appear weekly, and arriving early is the whole edge. Open on the funding event itself. Reach the founder or function owner through an open profile, make a small ask, and follow up twice with a new angle. A company that raised this month is deciding where the money goes; a company that raised eighteen months ago has already spent it.

Din runs LinkedIn + email outbound systems for B2B businesses at $10k/month or more and says he has worked with 140+ companies across 30 industries.

## Key Takeaways

- Recency of the funding event is the qualifier, not company fame — last month's raise still has unallocated budget
- YC, Techstars, Wellfound, and Product Hunt give you timestamps (batch, hire, launch) without a data subscription
- VC portfolio pages and regional EU directories are the least-worked sources because operators stop at Crunchbase
- EDGAR Form D plus risk factors give officer names and the company's own statement of what hurts
- Re-scrape weekly and open on the event; a one-shot dump of last year's rounds is already late
