Impeccable’s Slop Gallery catalogs 64 patterns that make an interface look AI-generated or poorly built, alongside 59 deterministic detector rules. Paul Bakaus presents it as a practical reference for recognizing recurring model defaults—such as overly editorial type choices and warm brown accents—before they flatten a product’s visual identity.

The gallery complements Impeccable’s emerging “anti-attractors” for fonts and colors: constraints intended to push generative design work away from familiar, fashionable defaults and toward more deliberately chosen visual directions.

## Key Takeaways

- The catalog names 64 UI anti-patterns associated with AI-generated or low-quality interfaces.
- Its 59 deterministic detector rules turn vague design discomfort into checkable signals.
- Identifying default typography, color, and layout tendencies is a prerequisite for producing a distinctive landing page or product interface.
- Anti-attractors offer a way to steer AI-assisted design away from common stylistic basins rather than merely replacing one set of defaults with another.
