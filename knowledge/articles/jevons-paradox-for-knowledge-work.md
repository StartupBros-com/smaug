Aaron Levie applies Jevons paradox to AI-enabled knowledge work: lowering the cost of work does not simply reduce the amount performed; it unlocks projects, experiments, and operational capabilities that were previously too expensive to attempt. He argues that agents can give small organizations access to functions once reserved for large enterprises, while humans remain responsible for framing workflows, providing context, and judging whether outputs create value.

## Key Takeaways

- AI's primary leverage is reducing the investment required to begin work, rather than merely increasing the return on a fixed amount of work.
- Cheaper capabilities can expand demand dramatically by making new software, research, marketing, and support initiatives viable for smaller teams.
- Agents can automate individual knowledge-work tasks, but reliable business outcomes still need human oversight, workflow design, and contextual judgment.
- As expectations rise with capability, jobs are likely to absorb newly possible tasks instead of disappearing in direct proportion to automation.
