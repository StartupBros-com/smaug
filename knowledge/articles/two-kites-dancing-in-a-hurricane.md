0xsmac argues that prediction markets have confused early traction with a product design that can reach mass adoption. The essay says binary, fully collateralized contracts fragment liquidity, leave many markets thin, and prevent efficient hedging and leverage; it proposes borrowing mechanisms from perpetual-futures exchanges instead.

## Key Takeaways

- Companies can mistake an early product's success for proof that its design remains suitable at a larger scale, as illustrated through several failed consumer platforms.
- Kalshi and Polymarket activity is concentrated in a small number of prominent events, while many other contracts have wide spreads and shallow order books.
- Liquidity rebates, volume rewards, and wash trading can inflate activity metrics without proving durable organic participation.
- Binary contracts split capital between outcomes, expose market makers directly to event risk, and do not safely support substantial leverage when prices can revalue suddenly.
- Full prepayment reduces counterparty risk but makes participation capital-intensive for both liquidity providers and traders.
- The proposed redesign uses continuous pricing, dynamic margin, leverage that declines near settlement, pooled or just-in-time collateral, and early scalar-market experiments.
