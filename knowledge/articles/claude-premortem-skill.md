Ole Lehmann adapts the pre-mortem technique for Claude: frame a plan as having already failed, then identify how it happened before investing the time or money to discover the failures in practice. The article argues that prospective hindsight counters an assistant's tendency to affirm a proposed plan by requiring concrete failure narratives instead.

The proposed skill gathers the plan's context, generates relevant failure modes, delegates an investigation of each one, and synthesizes the results into the likely and dangerous failures, hidden assumptions, a revised plan, and a pre-launch checklist. It is positioned for decisions where an incorrect commitment would impose substantial financial, professional, or personal costs.

## Key Takeaways

- A pre-mortem makes a team or agent reason backward from a declared failure, which can expose risks that an open-ended request for concerns misses.
- Treat failure modes as testable scenarios with underlying assumptions and observable early-warning signals.
- Use the synthesis to revise the plan and establish a short validation checklist before committing resources.
