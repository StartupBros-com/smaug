Anthropic’s research page investigates a global-workspace-like distinction inside Claude: much of a model’s internal activity may be present without being broadly available to the information processing used for describable, held-in-mind reasoning. The bookmark was shared in response to a question about J-space probes, placing the study in the context of mechanistic-interpretability tools for examining which internal representations are globally accessible.

The page’s framing draws an analogy to human cognition, where only a small fraction of neural activity is consciously accessible at a time. It presents this as an interpretability question about the organization of computation within a language model, rather than a claim that the model has human consciousness.

## Key Takeaways

- The research examines how broadly available information differs from other activity inside a language model.
- The global-workspace analogy offers a way to discuss information that can be maintained and used in reasoning.
- J-space probes are relevant as a practical interpretability lens for investigating internal representations related to this distinction.
