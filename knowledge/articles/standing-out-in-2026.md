Lulu Cheng Meservey argues that as AI-generated content, engagement, and polished corporate storytelling become harder to distinguish from one another, communications advantage will come from demonstrably real work. Rather than chasing reach alone, brands should make durable, high-quality things, show evidence of outcomes, create memorable real-world artifacts and events, and present imperfect human voices.

The article frames this as the next stage after companies first adopted direct CEO communication in 2024 and then competed for attention with video and content production in 2025. Its central claim is that authentic narratives cannot be manufactured in a campaign: they require months of consistent effort, relationships, craftsmanship, and results.

## Key Takeaways

- In a saturated field of synthetic content, proof, taste, and real-world consequences become narrative differentiators.
- Virality is not a sufficient objective when it erodes quality, dignity, or trust.
- Sustained execution over 6–18 months builds a more defensible brand narrative than a single launch moment.
