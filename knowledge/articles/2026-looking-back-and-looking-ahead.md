@apralky reflects on building a much larger online network in 2025 and proposes converting that reach into an organized intellectual movement. The essay treats online visibility as a route into elite and policy conversations, then sketches a staged effort to recruit participants, develop a worldview, build institutional ties, and influence public debate.

## Key Takeaways

- The author regards basic online network-building as complete after exceeding a 3,000-follower target by roughly tenfold.
- Online visibility is presented as a practical alternative to physical proximity to elite universities, technology hubs, and policy circles.
- The proposed strategy moves from recruitment and selective discussion spaces to institutional alliances, elite-opinion formation, and eventually funding and policy capacity.
- Effective Altruism is considered a useful organizational model, while its secular, universalist, and market-oriented philosophy is rejected as a complete answer for younger people.
- Rising geopolitical conflict, institutional distrust, and democratic decline are expected to create demand for more ideological and group-conscious frameworks.
- The immediate 2026 objective is to demonstrate tangible progress toward a durable coordinated organization rather than claim a finished institution.
