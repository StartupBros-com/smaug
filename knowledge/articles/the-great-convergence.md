Nicholas Charriere argues that software companies are converging on a common product architecture: a model harness receives a goal, calls tools in a loop, and persists until it produces an outcome. He frames this not as a uniform product fad, but as an economically rational contest to automate enterprise knowledge work.

The article connects application companies with proprietary workflow data, model providers with intelligence and cost-curve advantages, communication platforms that can coordinate humans and agents, and infrastructure vendors building the environments agents require. The durable advantage, Charriere says, will come from owning a shorter feedback cycle from observing an agent's performance to improving its code and context.

## Key Takeaways

- Agent products increasingly share a model-harness-tools loop, even when they originate in different categories such as productivity, coding, or systems of record.
- The critical capability is continuous improvement: agents can be monitored, evaluated, and revised through changes to their own harness code and context engineering.
- Existing product companies have distinct reasons to move toward agent platforms: workflow data, model capabilities, communication surfaces, or integrated infrastructure.
- Agent infrastructure is converging around sandboxes, computer-use environments, monitoring and evaluation, orchestration, storage, and version control.
- Competition may center on who owns the most complete agent lifecycle and can turn observations into reliable product improvements most quickly.
